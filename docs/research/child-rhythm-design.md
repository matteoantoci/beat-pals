# Child rhythm design: time skills, touch, and kid music games

## Summary for Beat Pals

- A 5-year-old child taps with a spread of about 90 to 120 ms around the beat and a mean error of up to about 100 ms (early at slow tempi). A symmetric hit window of ±250 ms catches about 90 to 99% of the taps of a child who follows the beat. Use ±250 ms, but never more than 45% of the gap to the next note. Use ±100 ms for an extra "great" reward. ([Einarson 2016, Table 1](https://prod-ms-be.lib.mcmaster.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/6335e706-9ff0-417d-a351-0b1c7e780570/content))
- Children of 4 to 5 years tap best near their own tempo (about 300 to 450 ms between taps). They follow 550 to 700 ms well, but they drift back to their own tempo. Above 1 s between notes, success falls below 50%. Keep the note gap between 500 and 750 ms, and never put two notes closer than 500 ms. ([McAuley 2006](https://taplab.psy.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/McAuley-Jones-Holub-Johnston-Miller_JEPGEN_2006.pdf), [Bobin-Bègue 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01346/full))
- Only half of the 4.5-year-olds synchronize with a beat from a speaker alone. With a live human partner, 10 of 12 synchronize. A visible drum machine gives no significant gain in accuracy. Co-play with a parent can help more than any animation. ([Kirschner 2009](http://web.archive.org/web/20240217191403id_/https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/Elsevier/Kirschner_Joint_JExpChildPsych_2009_1552981.pdf))
- On a touch screen, a child of 4 to 5 years can tap big targets well, but holds a tap for up to 4.2 s and fails two-hand multi-touch half of the time. Count the hit on touch start, ignore the touch duration, use no hold notes and no chords, and make each lane at least 2 cm wide. ([Vatavu 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/))
- Kid music games use no fail state, easy scores, many rewards, "strong beat only, any button" levels, and modes where the music waits for the child or the child sets the tempo. Guitar Hero World Tour "Beginner" is the closest model for the Beat Pals child mode. ([Wikipedia: Guitar Hero World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_Hero_World_Tour))

## 1. Time skills at age 4 to 5

### 1.1 Spontaneous motor tempo

Spontaneous motor tempo (SMT) is the interval between taps when a person taps at a comfortable rate, with no beat.

| Study | Age | Result |
|---|---|---|
| McAuley et al. 2006, n = 27 | 4 to 5 | SMT 312 ms (SE 16). Fastest tempo 262 ms. Slowest tempo 731 ms (SE 257). Adults (18 to 38): SMT 630 ms. ([PDF, Table 2](https://taplab.psy.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/McAuley-Jones-Holub-Johnston-Miller_JEPGEN_2006.pdf)) |
| McAuley et al. 2006 | 4 to 7 | "SMT shifts from approximately 300 ms for young children (ages 4–7) to near 700 ms for adults ages 75 and older." ([PDF](https://taplab.psy.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/McAuley-Jones-Holub-Johnston-Miller_JEPGEN_2006.pdf)) |
| McAuley et al. 2006, preferred perceptual tempo | 4 to 5 | The children rated two tempi as "just right": 1.0 × SMT and about 1.5 × SMT (about 470 ms). Other age groups had only one. ([PDF](https://taplab.psy.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/McAuley-Jones-Holub-Johnston-Miller_JEPGEN_2006.pdf)) |
| Provasi, Anderson & Barbu-Roth 2014 (review) | 4 and older | SMT "around 400–500 ms in children 4 years of age and older". It is more variable in young children. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4166894/)) |
| Provasi & Bobin-Bègue 2003 (from two later papers) | 2.5 and 4 | SMT about 400 ms. Children of 2.5 years synchronize only at 400 ms. Many 4-year-olds synchronize at 400, 600, and 800 ms. ([Kirschner 2009](http://web.archive.org/web/20240217191403id_/https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/Elsevier/Kirschner_Joint_JExpChildPsych_2009_1552981.pdf), [Provasi 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4166894/)). I could not read the 2003 paper itself. [UNVERIFIED for exact numbers] |
| Monier & Droit-Volet 2019, n = 57 | 5 to 7 | SMT mean 374 ms (SD 208). The coefficient of variation (CV) of the intervals is 0.16 to 0.19. Variability falls with age because fine motor skill improves. ([HAL preprint](https://hal.science/hal-05479658v1/document)) |
| Serré et al. 2026 (museum study) | 5 to 68 | CV of tap intervals "varies from 0.2 in the youngest participants to 0.07 in adults". ([Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38073-x)) |

The studies agree: at 4 to 5 years, the comfortable tap interval is about 300 to 450 ms (about 130 to 200 taps a minute). Individual children differ much (SD about 200 ms).

### 1.2 Tempo range for synchronization

- McAuley et al. estimated a stable "entrainment region" for each age group. For 4- to 5-year-olds, the upper limit is a beat interval of 506 ms. For adults, it is 759 ms. Above the limit, variability rises fast. ([PDF, Table 6](https://taplab.psy.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/McAuley-Jones-Holub-Johnston-Miller_JEPGEN_2006.pdf))
- In continuation (no beat), 4- to 5-year-olds produced 406 ms for a 506 ms target and 926 ms for a 1709 ms target. The youngest children "drift back to a rate near their SMT (≈ 300 ms) for all targets". ([PDF, Table 3](https://taplab.psy.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/McAuley-Jones-Holub-Johnston-Miller_JEPGEN_2006.pdf))
- Kirschner & Tomasello: 3.5- and 4.5-year-olds slowed down to a 600 ms beat in all three conditions. ([PDF](http://web.archive.org/web/20240217191403id_/https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/Elsevier/Kirschner_Joint_JExpChildPsych_2009_1552981.pdf))
- Einarson (n = 48 five-year-olds): the share of children with significant synchronization was 0.56 at 400 ms, 0.81 at 550 ms, and 0.79 at 700 ms. So 550 to 700 ms was easier than 400 ms for this group. ([thesis, Table 2](https://prod-ms-be.lib.mcmaster.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/6335e706-9ff0-417d-a351-0b1c7e780570/content))
- Bobin-Bègue et al.: 3- and 5-year-olds got a picture only when a tap came inside a window. At beat intervals above 1.0 s, "less than one tap out of two was reinforced". The 5-year-olds never produced intervals above about 1.5 s. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01346/full))
- Monier & Droit-Volet: at 700 and 900 ms, the younger children tapped too fast. At 500 ms, age had no effect, "probably due to the fact that the external tempo was close to the children's spontaneous tempo". ([HAL](https://hal.science/hal-05479658v1/document))
- Children speed up more easily than they slow down. ([Provasi 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4166894/), [Bobin-Bègue 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01346/full))

### 1.3 Accuracy and variability

Most child studies report circular statistics, not asynchrony in ms. R is the mean vector length (1 = all taps at the same phase, 0 = random). θ is the mean phase (0° = on the beat, negative = early).

Einarson, 5-year-olds, drum pad, metronome ([thesis, Table 1](https://prod-ms-be.lib.mcmaster.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/6335e706-9ff0-417d-a351-0b1c7e780570/content)):

| Beat interval | Mean R | Mean θ | θ in ms | Circular SD (derived) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400 ms | 0.38 | +6° | +7 ms | 80° = 89 ms |
| 550 ms | 0.58 | −51° | −78 ms | 60° = 91 ms |
| 700 ms | 0.56 | −54° | −105 ms | 62° = 120 ms |

I calculated the circular SD with the standard formula s = √(−2 ln R). The paper gives R as a mean of the R values of each child, so this SD is a rough group figure. 7-year-olds had R 0.59 to 0.74. [Derived]

Other data:

- Kirschner & Tomasello, 12 children for each age: the number of 4.5-year-olds with significant synchronization was 6 of 12 with a speaker, 9 of 12 with a visible drum machine, and 10 of 12 with a human partner (400 ms beat). At 600 ms: 6, 4, and 9 of 12. The mean asynchrony was "primarily positive" (late) at 400 ms. At 600 ms, it was mostly negative (early) for 3.5- and 4.5-year-olds. ([PDF, Table 1 and Fig. 4](http://web.archive.org/web/20240217191403id_/https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/Elsevier/Kirschner_Joint_JExpChildPsych_2009_1552981.pdf))
- Bobin-Bègue et al.: adults tap about 10 ms before the beat, but "this negative asynchrony is rarely found in young children". ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01346/full))
- Bobin-Bègue et al. used a reward window "from 15% before the auditory stimulus onset until the auditory stimulus offset" (sound 200 ms). At a 400 ms beat, that is −60 ms to +200 ms. In the phase after the 400 ms session (no sound, the picture reward stays), 72% of the taps got the reward, against 17% before the session. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01346/full))
- For comparison, adults: a trained musician can reach an asynchrony SD of 2% of the beat interval. For novices, it is "at least twice as large". Some adults tap 100 ms early. ([Repp 2005](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03206433.pdf)) At 500 ms, a novice adult has an SD of about 20 ms or more. A 5-year-old has about 90 ms, about 4 to 5 times wider. [Derived]
- Synchronization reaches adult level at about 8 to 10 years. ([Serré 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38073-x), [Monier 2019](https://hal.science/hal-05479658v1/document))

### 1.4 Real music compared with a metronome

- Einarson, 5- and 7-year-olds: "children were significantly better at tapping to metronomes than to songs". With 12 real music excerpts (88 to 172 BPM), 5-year-olds had R from 0.10 to 0.64. The share of children who synchronized went from 0.04 (Hawaiian song) to 0.88 (pop song). Spectral flux, energy, and density of the music predicted the result, not the meter. ([thesis](https://prod-ms-be.lib.mcmaster.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/6335e706-9ff0-417d-a351-0b1c7e780570/content))
- Kertész & Honbolygó, 6- to 7-year-olds (n = 37): taps were more consistent with a metronome, but the asynchrony was lower with music. ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.741540/full))
- Drake, Jones & Baruch tested 4-year-olds with simple sequences and music. With age, the synchronization rate slows: young children tap at a faster level of the meter. ([PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11018511/)). I could not read the full paper. [UNVERIFIED for exact numbers]
- Children of 1 to 4 years smile more with their favorite music, and they dance more with it at slow tempo (90 BPM). ([Kragness 2022, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35446066/))
- In adults, the negative mean asynchrony is "small or absent in musical contexts". ([Repp 2005](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03206433.pdf))

Result for Beat Pals: the beat in a real song is harder to follow than a click. The quality of the song matters much (clear drums help). The child likes a favorite song more. An optional soft click on the strong beats can help in the first sessions. [Inference]

### 1.5 Visual cues

- Adults synchronize much worse with a flash than with a sound. The fastest beat that adults can follow with a flash is about 460 ms. ([Repp 2005](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03206433.pdf))
- A visual target that moves and has a clear impact point (a ball that bounces on a floor) drives adult synchronization "nearly as accurately as sound". ([Iversen 2015, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25460395/), [Hove 2013, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22638726/))
- A tablet test in adults found the best results when the stimulus had both audio and visual information. ([Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02471/full))
- In young children, a video of a puppet that drums did not change the perception of beat errors, compared with static images. ([Einarson thesis abstract](https://prod-ms-be.lib.mcmaster.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/6335e706-9ff0-417d-a351-0b1c7e780570/content))
- Kirschner: the accuracy (R) with a visible drum machine was not different from the accuracy with a speaker. Only a human partner gave a significant gain. ([PDF](http://web.archive.org/web/20240217191403id_/https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/Elsevier/Kirschner_Joint_JExpChildPsych_2009_1552981.pdf))

Result for Beat Pals: a note that falls and hits a clear strike line is a cue that moves and has an impact point, so it can help. No study tests a note highway with 4-year-olds. The audio must stay the main cue. [Inference]

### 1.6 Social context

When the partner is a real person, children of all three ages synchronize with "higher accuracy", and 2.5-year-olds can slow down to 600 ms. ([Kirschner 2009, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18789454/)) Yu et al. found tempo adjustment to 600 ms from 24 months with a mother or a robot partner and a familiar song. ([Yu 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9558294/))

## 2. Proposed time parameters for the child mode

| Parameter | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Good" hit window | ±250 ms, and never more than 45% of the gap to the next or the previous note | 5-year-olds: SD 89 to 120 ms, bias −105 to +7 ms (section 1.3). If the error is normal, ±250 ms catches about 99% of taps at 400 ms, 97% at 550 ms, and 89% at 700 ms. [Derived] The 45% cap stops two windows from overlap. |
| "Great" hit window | ±100 ms | About 1 SD of a 5-year-old. It gives a learn signal: the child can hear and see a difference between "near" and "on". [Inference] |
| Window shape | Symmetric | The mean error is late at fast tempi and early at slow tempi (Kirschner 2009, Einarson 2016). |
| Offset for each child | Optional: shift the window center by the median error of the last 16 hits, limited to ±80 ms | Children have large individual bias (section 1.3). Device latency calibration is a separate topic (`ios-pwa-audio.md`). [Proposal] |
| Minimum note gap | 500 ms (120 notes a minute) | With a ±250 ms window, a smaller gap makes windows overlap. 5-year-olds synchronize worse at 400 ms than at 550 ms (Einarson). |
| Preferred note gap | 550 to 750 ms | Best synchronization share for 5-year-olds at 550 and 700 ms (Einarson). 4.5-year-olds adjust to 600 ms (Kirschner). |
| Upper note gap in a dense section | about 1000 ms | Above 1 s, under 50% of taps rewarded (Bobin-Bègue 2014). Longer rests between phrases are not a problem. [Inference] |
| Which beat level to chart | The level of the meter whose period is nearest to 550 to 750 ms: quarter notes at 80 to 110 BPM, half notes at 160 to 200 BPM | Young children follow a tempo near their own (section 1.2). |
| Extra taps | No penalty, no lost combo | Young children tap at a faster level of the meter (Drake 2000) and tap faster than the beat (Monier 2019). |
| Score of a hit | Time of touch start (`pointerdown`), not touch end | Touch duration of 4- to 5-year-olds goes up to 4.2 s (Vatavu 2015). |

## 3. Touch and motor skills at age 4 to 5

### 3.1 Target size and accuracy

- Nielsen Norman Group: "We recommend at least 2cm × 2cm touch targets for young children (4 times bigger than the 1cm x 1cm recommended target size for adult users)." ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/))
- Apple HIG: "a button needs a hit region of at least 44x44 pt". This is the adult minimum. ([Apple HIG, Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons))
- Vatavu et al. (89 children of 3 to 6 years): mean tap offset from the target center was 4.5 mm at 3 years, 3.8 mm at 4 years, and 3.4 mm above 5 years. Adults: 2.1 to 3.3 mm. The study accepted a tap inside a 23 mm circle. The authors say to accept "target offsets of 10 mm for 3-year-olds". ([PDF](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf))
- Children were more accurate on a 3.5-inch phone (offset 3.3 mm) than on a 10.1-inch tablet (4.5 mm). Task completion was 90.8% on the phone and 72.8% on the tablet. The targets were 8 mm on the phone and 20 mm on the tablet. ([PDF](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf))

For Beat Pals: three lanes on an iPhone in landscape (screen about 14 cm wide) are about 4.5 cm each, far above 2 cm. Make the full lane column the target, not only the strike line. [Inference]

### 3.2 Tap, hold, swipe, multi-touch

- Tap: 98.7% completion. Double tap: 82.8%. One-finger drag: 92.0%. Two-hand multi-touch drag: 53.7% (30.7% on the tablet). The authors say: "Avoid multi-touch interactions or use them with caution." ([Vatavu 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf))
- Hold: tap duration went up to 5.1 s under 4 years, 4.2 s at 4 to 5 years, and 1.5 s above 5 years. Children "don't know how much to keep their fingers on the surface". ([Vatavu 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf))
- Other fingers and the palm: in 8.3% (phone) and 17% (tablet) of cases, children did not use the index finger. The authors say to accept other fingers, more fingers, and other hand poses as correct input. ([Vatavu 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf))
- Children of 2 to 4 years can all tap and slide. Pinch and spread fail often (spread 11% at 2 years). (Abdul Aziz et al. 2013, from [Vatavu 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf))
- NN/g: "Touchscreen designs for kids under 9 should emphasize swiping, tapping, and dragging." And: "Design for kids under 5 should not require complex motor coordination such as two-handed use or quick manual actions in response to a visual stimulus." A 5-year-old failed a game that needed two hands at the same time. ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/))
- Precise drag to a small spot frustrates children: "I don't like this game. Don't ask me why." (5-year-old). ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/))
- Children touch again near the old target after it moves ("holdovers"). (Anthony 2012, from [Vatavu 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf))
- iOS Safari does not support `navigator.vibrate`, so a PWA on iPhone has no haptic feedback. ([MDN browser-compat-data](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/api/Navigator.json))

For Beat Pals:

1. One finger, one tap for each note. No hold notes, no chords, no swipes in the child mode.
2. A touch with more than one contact point in a lane counts as one tap.
3. Lane changes are a "quick manual action in response to a visual stimulus". Keep the same lane for a phrase. Change the lane only on a long gap. [Inference from NN/g]
4. First level: tap anywhere on the screen (like Guitar Hero "Beginner", section 4). Then 3 lanes. [Proposal]

### Patterns

1. **No fail state.** Clone Hero (default), Rock Band 2 (option), Just Dance Kids Mode, and all preschool music toys keep the song to the end.
2. **Strong beats only, any input.** Guitar Hero "Beginner" charts the bass drum beats and accepts any button. This is the closest match to the Beat Pals child mode.
3. **Fewer lanes and fewer notes.** Guitar Hero "Easy" uses 3 of 5 frets and fewer notes.
4. **The music follows the child.** Sago Mini Music Box: each tap plays the next note, so the child sets the tempo. Synthesia waits for the correct note.
5. **No wrong notes.** Toca Band (C major) and BANDIMAL (pentatonic) make every input sound good.
6. **Gentle prompts, not error sounds.** Elmo's characters remind the child of the goal.
7. **Adult help.** Elmo lets a parent take over with a second controller.
8. **Skip after repeated fails.** Rhythm Heaven offers a skip after 3 fails.
9. **A show as the reward.** PEG + CAT Big Gig: after the song, the characters perform.
10. **Familiar songs.** Simply Piano kids track and the Kragness 2022 data.
11. **Contingent reward.** In the lab, a picture appears only after a tap in time. 3- to 5-year-olds learn from it.
12. **Instrument mute on failure.** Rock Band mutes a failed player. For a 4-year-old, a full mute is a punishment. Beat Pals must soften it (section 5).

## 5. Feedback without failure: ideas

These are proposals. Each one names its basis.

1. **The song never stops.** No fail meter, no "Try Again" screen. Basis: patterns 1 and 6.
2. **The instrument stays audible.** On a miss, lower the instrument stem by a small amount (about 6 dB), and bring it back to full level on the next hit. Never mute it. A second option: keep the stem at full level all the time, and add a bright accent sample (drum hit, sparkle) only on a hit. Basis: pattern 12 shows the punitive form. The README asks this as open question 4.
3. **A hit gives a sound and a picture together.** On a hit, play a short accent sound and show an animation at the strike line. On a "great" hit (±100 ms), add a bigger effect. Basis: contingent reward (pattern 11), reward animations kept 3- to 6-year-olds engaged ([Vatavu 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf)). Gamification raised completion in 5- to 7-year-olds from 73% to 97% (Brewer et al. 2013, from [Vatavu 2015](https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf)).
4. **A miss gives no sound.** No buzzer, no "clank", no red cross. The note fades away. Basis: pattern 6.
5. **Extra taps are free.** A tap outside every window plays a soft note of the instrument in key, like Toca Band. No combo to lose. Basis: patterns 5 and section 2 ("Extra taps").
6. **No numeric score for the child.** Show stars or stickers at the end of every song, with a minimum of one star. Basis: Just Dance Kids Mode "easy scoring and increased rewards". The README asks this as open question 5.
7. **A show at the end.** The band of characters plays the last bars of the song while stickers appear. Basis: pattern 9.
8. **Silent adaptive difficulty.** If the child hits less than half of the last 16 notes, remove notes (keep only the downbeats) and widen the window up to its cap. If the child hits more than 85%, add notes back. Do not show the level change. Basis: patterns 2, 3, and 8, and the large individual differences in section 1. Thresholds are a guess. [Proposal]
9. **A wait mode for the first sessions.** The next note waits at the strike line until the child taps, then the song continues. This works only with a click track or a stem that can pause. With a full song file, the audio must pause and start again, and that can sound bad. Basis: pattern 4. [Proposal; audio feasibility is in `ios-pwa-audio.md`]
10. **Co-play with a parent.** A parent taps one lane and the child taps another, or the parent taps the same beat on the same screen. Basis: Kirschner 2009 (a human partner helps, a machine does not) and Elmo's second remote. NN/g warns about two-hand use by one child, not about two people. [Proposal]
11. **A warm-up.** Before the song, the child taps along with 8 clicks at the song tempo, with a friendly character. Use the taps to measure the child's offset (section 2). Basis: PEG + CAT "warm-ups", Bobin-Bègue 2014 (children improved over the first session). [Proposal]

## Open questions

1. No study gives asynchrony in ms for 4-year-olds on a glass touch screen. The lab data come from drum pads, plates over a monitor, and keyboards. Beat Pals can log the offset of each tap and check the ±250 ms window with the real child.
2. How fast must notes fall on the highway, and how many beats of look-ahead does a 4-year-old need? I found no source. Synthesia only says to play "slowly enough that you're comfortable with how quickly notes arrive".
3. Does a note highway help a 4-year-old, or does it pull attention from the sound? Cues that move help adults (Iversen 2015). No child data.
4. Can an animated character give some of the "social partner" benefit? Kirschner found no benefit from a visible drum machine.
5. iPhone or iPad (README question 1)? Children were more accurate and completed more tasks on a 3.5-inch phone than on a 10.1-inch tablet (Vatavu 2015). But an iPad gives bigger lanes and a bigger strike line. Test both.
6. How long can a song be? Rhythm Heaven games last 1 to 2 minutes. No data for this age group.
7. Is a 6 dB drop on a miss audible enough to teach, but soft enough not to upset the child? Test with the child.
8. Drake, Jones & Baruch (2000) and Provasi & Bobin-Bègue (2003) are behind paywalls. Their exact numbers for 4-year-olds with music are not in this file.
